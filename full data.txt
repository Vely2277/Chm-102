EXAMINATION REVISION 
SOLUTIONS 
1. A) Calcination
Explanation:
Calcination is not a method of ore 
concentration (purification). Instead, 
it is a metallurgical process in which 
an ore is heated strongly in the 
absence or limited supply of air to 
remove moisture, carbon dioxide, or 
other volatile impurities. Methods of 
concentrating ores include gravity 
separation, froth flotation, magnetic 
separation, and leaching.
2. B) He said that the Octave rule 
works for all elements including the 
ones with heavier mass numbers
Explanation:
Newlands arranged elements in order 
of increasing atomic mass and 
observed that every eighth element 
had properties similar to the first, 
just like notes in a musical octave. 
However, this pattern did not hold 
for heavier elements, which is why 
his theory had significant limitations. 
His work nevertheless laid the 
foundation for the development of 
the modern periodic table.
3. A) Ethanoic acid > Ethanol > 
Ethanal > Ethanone > Ethanoate
Explanation:
Ethanoic acid is the strongest acid 
among the compounds listed because 
it forms a resonance-stabilized 
carboxylate ion after donating a 
proton. Ethanol is only weakly acidic 
due to its hydroxyl group. Aldehydes 
and ketones have no O–H bond and 
are much less acidic, while the 
ethanoate ion is already the conjugate 
base of ethanoic acid and therefore 
has essentially no acidic proton to 
donate.
4. A) They are produced by the 
alkaline hydrolysis of alkanoates
Explanation:
Esters undergo alkaline hydrolysis 
(saponification) when heated with 
sodium hydroxide or potassium 
hydroxide. This reaction produces an 
alkanoate salt and an alcohol.
Example:
CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + 
C₂H₅OH
Acid hydrolysis, on the other hand, 
produces a carboxylic acid rather 
than its salt.
5. D) Propan-1-ol and Propan-2-ol
Explanation:
Hydrogenation reduces the carbonyl 
group (C=O) to an alcohol.
● Propanal (an aldehyde) is reduced 
to propan-1-ol, a primary alcohol.
● Propanone (a ketone) is reduced 
to propan-2-ol, a secondary 
alcohol.
This reduction can be carried out 
using hydrogen with a nickel catalyst 
or reducing agents such as sodium 
borohydride (NaBH₄) or lithium 
aluminium hydride (LiAlH₄).
6. C) Potassium tetrachlorocuprate 
(II)
Explanation:
The complex ion [CuCl₄]²⁻ carries a 
negative charge, so the metal name 
changes from copper to cuprate. 
Since there are four chloride ligands, 
the prefix tetrachloro- is used. 
Copper has an oxidation state of +2, 
giving the correct IUPAC name 
potassium tetrachlorocuprate(II).
7. C) Be
Explanation:
Although beryllium belongs to Group 
2 (alkaline earth metals), it differs 
significantly from the other members 
because beryllium oxide (BeO) and 
beryllium hydroxide [Be(OH)₂] are 
amphoteric. This means they react 
with both acids and bases. The other 
Group 2 metals (Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) 
generally form basic oxides and 
hydroxides. This unusual behavior is 
due to the small ionic size and high 
charge density of Be²⁺
.
8. C) I
Explanation:
Atomic size generally increases down 
a group because additional electron 
shells are added, increasing the 
distance between the nucleus and the 
outermost electrons. Iodine (I) lies 
much lower in the periodic table than 
fluorine (F), oxygen (O), and sodium 
(Na), so it possesses the largest 
atomic orbital (largest atomic radius) 
among the options.
9. C) Mg
Explanation:
Alkali metals (Group 1) have one 
valence electron, which is easily 
removed, giving them very low 
ionization energies. Sodium (Na), 
potassium (K), and francium (Fr) all 
belong to Group 1. Magnesium (Mg), 
however, belongs to Group 2 and has 
two valence electrons. Removing its 
first electron requires more energy 
because its electron configuration is 
more stable, making it the exception.
10. B) Cr, Mn, Ni, Cu
Explanation:
The first-row transition elements are 
arranged in the following order:
Sc → Ti → V → Cr → Mn → Fe → Co → Ni → 
Cu → Zn
Therefore, the missing elements after 
Ti and V are Cr and Mn, while those 
after Co are Ni and Cu. This sequence 
follows increasing atomic number 
across the first transition series.
11. D) Different structures and 
different properties
Explanation:
Allotropy is the existence of an 
element in two or more different 
structural forms in the same physical 
state. Because the arrangement of 
atoms differs, the allotropes also 
possess different physical and 
sometimes chemical properties.
Examples:
● Carbon: diamond, graphite, 
graphene, fullerenes.
● Oxygen: O₂ and O₃ (ozone).
Different structures lead to 
differences in hardness, conductivity, 
density, and reactivity.
12. B) High affinity for O₂
Explanation:
Highly reactive metals such as 
sodium, magnesium, calcium, and 
aluminium form very stable oxides 
because they have a strong attraction 
for oxygen. Carbon cannot remove 
oxygen from these oxides during 
reduction because carbon has a lower 
affinity for oxygen than these metals. 
Consequently, these metals are 
extracted by electrolysis instead of 
chemical reduction with carbon.
13. C) Zn
Explanation:
Potassium, sodium, and magnesium 
are highly electropositive metals and 
are extracted by electrolytic 
reduction of their molten salts. Zinc 
is less reactive and can be extracted 
by chemical reduction with carbon 
(coke) after roasting its ore. 
Therefore, zinc is the metal that does 
not require electrolytic reduction.
14. A) Mg
Explanation:
Magnesium lies above carbon in the 
reactivity series, meaning its oxide 
(MgO) is extremely stable. Carbon 
cannot reduce magnesium oxide 
because magnesium has a greater 
affinity for oxygen than carbon. 
Instead, magnesium is extracted by 
the electrolysis of molten magnesium 
chloride. Iron, tin, and lead oxides, 
however, can be reduced using 
carbon or carbon monoxide.
15. A) Same bond
Explanation:
Hybrid orbitals are formed by the 
mixing of atomic orbitals of similar 
energy. All hybrid orbitals produced 
are equivalent—they have the same 
energy, the same size, and the same 
shape. However, they do not 
represent the “same bond.” Instead, 
hybrid orbitals participate in forming 
different sigma (σ) bonds depending 
on the atoms they overlap with. 
Therefore, “same bond” is not a 
property of hybrid orbitals.
16. D) Chemical properties
Explanation:
Elements in the same group have the 
same number of valence electrons, 
giving them similar chemical 
properties. For example, Group 1 
elements all have one valence 
electron and readily lose it to form +1 
ions. While some physical properties 
(such as melting point and density) 
show trends within a group, they are 
not identical, so the best answer is 
chemical properties.
17. A) There is a visible drift of 
electron
Explanation:
The inductive effect is the permanent 
displacement of sigma (σ) electrons 
along a carbon chain due to 
differences in electronegativity 
between bonded atoms. This 
displacement is not visible and occurs 
only through sigma bonds. It results 
in unequal sharing of electrons in a 
covalent bond and influences 
properties such as acidity, basicity, 
and the stability of reaction 
intermediates.
18. D) Decomposition
Explanation:
Nuclear reactions involve changes in 
the atomic nucleus. Examples include 
fusion (combining light nuclei), 
fission (splitting heavy nuclei), and 
transmutation (conversion of one 
element into another through nuclear 
changes). Decomposition is an 
ordinary chemical reaction in which a 
compound breaks into simpler 
substances without altering the 
nucleus of its atoms.
19. D) S- and D-block elements
Explanation:
Transition metals occupy the d-block 
of the periodic table and lie between 
the s-block elements (Groups 1 and 2) 
and the p-block elements (Groups 13–
18). Historically, they are described 
as forming a transition from the 
highly electropositive s-block metals 
to the p-block elements. This position 
explains their intermediate 
properties, such as variable oxidation 
states and complex ion formation.
Note: Although option D says “S- and D￾block elements,” the accepted intention of 
the question is that transition elements lie 
immediately after the s-block and begin 
the d-block.
20. B) Calcination
Explanation:
Calcination is the process of heating 
an ore in the absence or limited 
supply of air. It is mainly used for 
carbonate and hydrated ores, 
removing volatile substances such as

●Lone pair–Lone pair (strongest)
●Lone pair–Bond pair
●Bond pair–Bond pair (weakest)

pyramidal, and boron trifluoride

carbon chain contains five carbon 
atoms (pentane). A methyl (CH₃)

substituents the lowest possible

89. B. HBr to alkenes
Explanation: Markovnikov’s rule 
states that when an unsymmetrical 
reagent such as HX (e.g., HBr, HCl) 
adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the 
hydrogen atom attaches to the carbon 
already bearing more hydrogen 
atoms, while the halide attaches to 
the more substituted carbon. This 
produces the more stable carbocation 
intermediate and therefore the major 
product.
90. C. Bromine water
Explanation: Bromine water is used 
to test for unsaturation (carbon￾carbon double or triple bonds). An

the orange/brown bromine solution

multiple bond. Saturated 
hydrocarbons (alkanes) do not react 
under these conditions, so the colour 
remains unchanged.
91. B. Aldehydes and/or ketones
Explanation: Ozonolysis is the 
cleavage of a carbon–carbon double

reductive work-up (e.g., Zn/H₂O), 
each carbon of the double bond 
becomes part of a carbonyl group, 
producing aldehydes, ketones, or 
both, depending on the structure of 
the original alkene. Ozonolysis is 
widely used to determine the position 
of double bonds in alkenes.
92. A. sp

● One σ bond with the other carbon,
● One σ bond with hydrogen,
●Two π bonds between the carbon 
atoms.

undergoes sp hybridization, giving a 
linear geometry with a bond angle of 
180°.

CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃CHO → CH₃COOH

● X = halogen (Cl, Br, or I).

organometallic compounds and act as

102. A. Ammonia
Explanation: Amines are organic 
derivatives of ammonia (NH₃) in

●Secondary amine: R₂NH

103. B. Cl⁻

nucleophile.

●H⁺ is an electrophile.

104. C. Both substrate and 
nucleophile
Explanation: An SN2 (bimolecular 
nucleophilic substitution) reaction 
occurs in a single step where the 
nucleophile attacks the substrate as 
the leaving group departs 
simultaneously.
The rate law is:
Rate = k[substrate][nucleophile]
Thus, increasing the concentration of 
either the substrate or the 
nucleophile increases the reaction 
rate. This is why SN2 reactions are 
called second-order reactions.

Explanation: An electrophile is an 
electron-deficient species that 
accepts an electron pair during a 
reaction. The nitronium ion (NO₂⁺)

aromatic substitution.
106. B. Benzene
Explanation: C₆H₆ is benzene, the 
simplest aromatic hydrocarbon. It 
consists of a six-membered carbon 
ring with delocalized π electrons 
spread evenly over the ring. This 
delocalization gives benzene 
exceptional stability, known as 
aromaticity, causing it to undergo 
substitution reactions rather than 
addition reactions.
107. D. All of the above

Explanation: Nitrogen gas (N₂) is not

symmetrical diatomic molecule with

113. A. C₆₀

114. A. Diels–Alder reaction
Explanation: The Diels–Alder

reaction involving the concerted 
cycloaddition of a conjugated diene

intermediates and is widely used in 
organic synthesis to construct cyclic

115. A. Hydrogenation
Explanation: Hydrogenation is the 
addition of hydrogen (H₂) across a

palladium (Pd).

CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃

food industry to harden vegetable 
oils.

● CH₄ has no lone pair available for

Lewis acid.
117. A. Methanoic acid
Explanation: HCOOH is the IUPAC 
name for methanoic acid, the 
simplest member of the carboxylic 
acid family. It is also commonly

118. C. Sucrose
Explanation: Sucrose is a non￾reducing sugar because both of its

In contrast, glucose, fructose, and

Explanation: Fermentation is the 
biochemical process in which

conditions.

C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
This process is widely used in 
brewing, baking, and the industrial

Explanation: Saponification is the

Explanation: The monomer of 
polyethene is ethene (CH₂=CH₂).

long-chain polymer.
n(CH2=CH2) ——-> (-CH2-CH2-)n
No small molecule is eliminated

123. B. Nylon
Explanation: Nylon is a condensation 
polymer formed by the reaction of a 
diamine and a dicarboxylic acid (or

elimination of small molecules such

formed from unsaturated monomers 
without the loss of any small 
molecule.
124. B. O₃
Explanation: Ozone is a triatomic 
allotrope of oxygen with the 
molecular formula O₃. It forms 
naturally in the stratosphere, where 
it absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV)

respiratory system.
125. C. Solar energy
Explanation: Solar energy is a 
renewable energy source because 
sunlight is continuously supplied by

finite radioactive fuels such as 
uranium and is therefore not 
classified as renewable.
126. B. 7

[H^+] = [OH^-] = 1.0 * 10^-7mol L^-1

●Temperature
● Concentration of reactants
●Surface area
● Pressure (for gases)
● Catalysts

129. A. Equilibrium systems
Explanation: Le Chatelier’s principle 
states that when a system at 
equilibrium is subjected to a change 
in concentration, temperature, or 
pressure, the system adjusts in a 
direction that opposes the imposed 
change to establish a new 
equilibrium. This principle is widely 
applied in industrial processes such 
as the Haber process and the Contact 
process to maximize product yield.
130. B. s⁻¹
Explanation: For a first-order 
reaction, the rate law is:
Rate = k[A]

and concentration has units of mol 
L⁻¹, the units of the rate constant (k)

s^-1
This distinguishes first-order 
reactions from second-order and 
zero-order reactions, which have 
different units for their rate 
constants.

of an alkene, producing an alcohol. 
For example, ethene reacts with 
steam in the presence of an acid 
catalyst to form ethanol.
19. C (But-2-ene)
Explanation: Geometrical (cis-trans 
or E-Z) isomerism occurs when each 
carbon atom of the double bond is 
attached to two different groups. But-
2-ene satisfies this requirement and 
therefore exists as cis- and trans￾isomers.
20. C (Carbonyl carbon)
Explanation: The carbonyl carbon is 
always sp² hybridized because it 
forms three sigma bonds and one pi 
bond. This gives it a trigonal planar 
geometry. In contrast, alkane carbons 
are sp³ hybridized, alkene carbons 
are sp² only if they are part of the 
double bond, and alcohol carbons are 
usually sp³ hybridized.
21. B. Alkanamides
The –CONH₂ functional group is the 
amide (alkanamide) functional 
group. It contains both a carbonyl 
group (C=O) and an amino group (–
NH₂). Carboxylic acids contain –
COOH, oximes contain C=N–OH, 
while amines contain only –NH₂ 
without a carbonyl group.
22. C. Ethanoic acid
Ethanoic acid reacts with PCl₅ to 
form ethanoyl chloride, releasing HCl 
fumes, and also reacts with 
NaOH(aq) in a neutralization 
reaction to form sodium ethanoate 
and water. Ethanol reacts with PCl₅ 
but not with NaOH, while ethene does 
not react with either under normal 
conditions.
23. B. Ethanol → ethanal
Oxidation of ethanol to ethanal 
increases the oxidation number of 
carbon by adding oxygen (or 
removing hydrogen) without 
breaking any carbon–carbon bond. 
Dehydration of ethanol to ethene is 
an elimination reaction, not 
oxidation.
24. C. Alkanals
The carbonyl carbon in aldehydes is 
sp² hybridized, giving the carbonyl 
group a trigonal planar arrangement. 
Alkanes and alcohol carbons are 
generally sp³ hybridized, while ethers 
do not possess a planar carbonyl 
group.
25. C. Hydroxyethanoic acid
Hydroxyethanoic acid contains two 
different functional groups: a 
hydroxyl group (–OH) and a 
carboxylic acid group (–COOH). The 
other compounds each contain only 
one principal functional group.
26. C. Ethene
Nucleophilic addition is 
characteristic of carbonyl compounds 
because the carbonyl carbon is 
electrophilic. Ethene mainly 
undergoes electrophilic addition, not 
nucleophilic addition.
27. B. Acidity
Moving from alkanes → alcohols → 
carboxylic acids, acidity increases 
dramatically because the ability to donate 
a proton becomes much greater. Alkanes 
are essentially non-acidic, alcohols are 
weak acids, and carboxylic acids are 
significantly stronger due to resonance 
stabilization of the carboxylate ion.
28. B. Ethanal
Ethanal contains a carbonyl group 
that reacts with hydroxylamine to 
form an oxime. It does not originally 
contain an –OH group; the –OH in 
the oxime comes from hydroxylamine 
during the reaction.
29. B. Decomposition
Fusion, fission, transmutation, and 
radioactive decay all involve changes 
in the atomic nucleus and are 
therefore nuclear reactions. Ordinary 
decomposition is a chemical reaction, 
involving only the rearrangement of 
electrons and chemical bonds.
30. D. Be
Although beryllium belongs to Group 
IIA, its oxide (BeO) and hydroxide 
[Be(OH)₂] are amphoteric, reacting 
with both acids and bases. This 
unusual behavior makes beryllium 
chemically distinct from the other 
alkaline earth metals.
31. D. Most nuclei also contain a 
neutral particle called neutrons
James Chadwick discovered the 
neutron in 1932, proving that atomic 
nuclei contain neutral particles called 
neutrons in addition to protons. The 
other statements describe 
contributions from Dalton or 
Rutherford.
32. E. [Ar] 4s² 3d⁸
Nickel has atomic number 28, giving 
the ground-state electron 
configuration [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s². The 
options using [Kr] are incorrect 
because krypton is a much heavier 
noble gas.
33. C. Tetrahedral
A carbon atom with sp³ hybridization 
forms four equivalent hybrid orbitals 
directed toward the corners of a 
tetrahedron, producing bond angles 
of approximately 109.5°, as in 
methane.
34. A. Blue
A complex appears the 
complementary color of the light it 
absorbs. Since 600 nm corresponds 
to orange light, the observed color is 
blue, its complementary color.
35. D. S- and d-block elements
Transition metals occupy the d-block 
and lie between the highly reactive s￾block elements and the p-block 
elements. Hence they are described 
as a bridge beginning after the s￾block.
36. B. 4.90
Cr²⁺ has the electronic configuration 3d⁴, 
containing four unpaired electrons. Using 
the spin-only formula, μ = √[n(n+2)] = √24 ≈ 
4.90 BM.
37. B. 120°
In sp² hybridization, three hybrid 
orbitals arrange themselves in a 
trigonal planar geometry with bond
angles of approximately 120°.
38. B. S
As the s-character of a hybrid orbital 
increases (sp > sp² > sp³), electrons 
are held closer to the nucleus, 
resulting in larger bond angles and 
stronger bonds.
39. A. Shorter and stronger
Greater s-character brings bonding 
electrons closer to the nucleus, 
producing bonds that are shorter, 
stronger, and less easily broken.
40. B. Two hybrid orbitals
Mixing one s orbital with one p 
orbital produces two equivalent sp 
hybrid orbitals, leaving two 
unhybridized p orbitals available for 
π-bond formation.
41. C. Froth flotation method
Froth flotation is the standard 
method used to concentrate sulphide 
ores. Finely powdered ore is mixed 
with water, oil, and air. Sulphide 
particles attach to air bubbles and 
rise to the surface as froth, while the 
gangue sinks because it is not wetted 
by the oil.
42. B. Bauxite
Bauxite is the principal ore of 
aluminium. It mainly contains 
hydrated aluminium oxides and is 
first purified by the Bayer process 
before aluminium is extracted by 
electrolysis in the Hall–Héroult 
process.
43. B. Highly reactive nature
Although the question mentions 
Group IIA, the storage method 
described actually applies mainly to 
Group IA (alkali) metals. These 
metals are stored under paraffin oil 
or an inert atmosphere because they 
react rapidly with oxygen and 
moisture in air due to their highly 
reactive nature.
44. B. Calcination
Calcination is the heating of ores in 
the absence or limited supply of air. It 
is mainly used for carbonate and 
hydrated ores to remove volatile 
substances such as carbon dioxide 
and water. Roasting, in contrast, is 
carried out in excess air.
45. B. N³⁻ > O²⁻ > F⁻ > Ne > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺
These species are all isoelectronic (10 
electrons each). In an isoelectronic 
series, ionic radius decreases as 
nuclear charge increases because the 
same number of electrons is attracted 
more strongly by a greater number of 
protons.
46. B. 1.13209 × 10⁻²⁷ kg
Mass defect is obtained by 
subtracting the actual nuclear mass 
from the total mass of the separate 
protons and neutrons. The value in 
atomic mass units is then converted 
to kilograms using 1 amu = 1.6605655 
× 10⁻²⁷ kg.
47. C. CN⁻
The cyanide ion (CN⁻) is one of the 
strongest ligands in the 
spectrochemical series. It produces a 
large crystal field splitting energy (Δ), 
often causing electron pairing and 
the formation of low-spin complexes.
48. D. Sc³⁺
A transition metal ion must possess a 
partially filled d-subshell. Sc³⁺ has the 
electronic configuration 3d⁰, 
meaning its d-orbitals are completely 
empty, so it does not satisfy the 
IUPAC definition of a transition 
metal ion.
49. D. The higher the number of 
substituents with –I effect, the higher 
the acidity of the carboxylic acid
This statement is the least accurate 
because acidity depends not only on 
the number of electron-withdrawing 
(–I) groups but also on their strength 
and their position relative to the 
carboxyl group. The other statements 
are correct.
50. D. None of the above
“Methyl-ethanol” refers to 2-
propanol (isopropanol). Oxidation 
with acidified potassium dichromate 
converts it into propanone (acetone), 
which is not listed among the options.
51. D. Carbon nanotubes are 
cylindrical in shape
Carbon nanotubes are cylindrical 
structures formed by rolling 
graphene sheets into tubes. They 
possess exceptional mechanical 
strength, thermal stability, and 
electrical conductivity.
52. A. Covalent
The carbon atoms in fullerenes are 
joined by strong covalent bonds. Each 
carbon is approximately sp² 
hybridized, producing a stable cage￾like structure.
53. A. CH₃I and IC₂H₅
Excess concentrated HI cleaves 
ethers completely. Methoxyethane 
first produces methyl iodide and 
ethanol, and the ethanol 
subsequently reacts with HI to give 
ethyl iodide, yielding CH₃I and 
C₂H₅I.
54. C. 2,2-Dimethyl-3-butene
Following IUPAC rules, the longest 
chain containing the double bond is 
selected and numbered 
appropriately. The structure is 
therefore named 2,2-dimethylbut-3-
ene, represented in the options as 
2,2-Dimethyl-3-butene.
55. C. Iodide
In Lassaigne’s test, iodide ions react 
with silver nitrate to produce a yellow 
precipitate of silver iodide (AgI), 
which is insoluble in ammonia. 
Chloride gives a white precipitate, 
while bromide produces a cream 
precipitate.
56. A. Hybridization involves mixing 
of atomic orbitals to form new 
different orbitals
Hybridization produces equivalent 
(identical) hybrid orbitals, not 
different ones. The remaining 
statements are correct: alkynes have 
shorter bonds than alkenes, sp² bond 
angles are 120°, and sp orbitals have 
greater s-character than sp² orbitals.
57. A. C₂H₆O
Taking 100 g of the compound:
●Carbon = 52.18/12 ≈ 4.35 mol 
●Hydrogen = 13.04/1 ≈ 13.04 mol 
●Oxygen = 34.78/16 ≈ 2.17 mol 
Dividing each by 2.17 gives

58. A. 1-Pentene
Ozonolysis splits the C=C bond. 
Formation of methanal (HCHO) 
indicates that one carbon of the 
double bond was terminal (CH₂=), 
while the other fragment becomes 
propanal, identifying the alkene as 1-
pentene.
59. A. Primary
Cyclopropanol is a primary alcohol 
because the carbon atom bearing the 
hydroxyl group (–OH) is attached to 
only one other carbon atom. Alcohols 
are classified according to the 
number of carbon atoms bonded to 
the carbon carrying the –OH group.
60. A. Amines are basic, acidic, and 
neutral
Amines are generally basic because 
the nitrogen atom has a lone pair of 
electrons that readily accepts a 
proton (H⁺). They are not acidic 
under normal conditions. Amines 
also react with acids such as HCl to 
form ammonium salts.
61. B. II & III
Ketones do not form intermolecular 
hydrogen bonds among themselves 
because they lack an O–H bond. 
Therefore, they have lower boiling 
points than alcohols and carboxylic 
acids, both of which exhibit strong 
intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
62. C. Butyl propyl ether
This reaction is an example of 
Williamson ether synthesis, where an 
alkoxide reacts with a primary alkyl 
halide to form an ether. Butanol and 
iodopropane produce butyl propyl 
ether.
63. C. They attack centres of positive 
charge
Electrophiles are electron-deficient 
species that seek electron-rich 
(negative) centres. They do not attack 
positively charged centres; instead, 
they accept electron pairs from 
nucleophiles.
64. C. Propanal > Propanone > 
Benzaldehyde > Acetophenone
Nucleophilic addition is fastest in 
aldehydes because they have less 
steric hindrance and a more 
electrophilic carbonyl carbon than 
ketones. Aromatic carbonyl 
compounds are less reactive due to 
resonance stabilization with the 
benzene ring.
65. A. 2-Hexene
Geometrical (cis–trans or E/Z) 
isomerism occurs only when each 
carbon of the double bond is attached 
to two different groups. This 
condition is satisfied in 2-hexene.
66. D. 2-Hydroxy-4-chlorohexanoic 
acid
In carboxylic acids, numbering 
begins from the carboxyl carbon (C-
1). This places the hydroxyl group at 
carbon 2 and the chlorine atom at 
carbon 4, giving 2-hydroxy-4-
chlorohexanoic acid.
67. B. A catalyst increases reaction 
rate by increasing activation energy
Catalysts actually lower the activation 
energy, providing an alternative 
reaction pathway. This allows a 
greater proportion of reacting 
molecules to possess sufficient 
energy for successful collisions.
68. D. Propanamide and ethanoic 
acid
Ammonia reacts with acid anhydrides 
to produce an amide and a carboxylic 
acid. From the compounds given, the 
products correspond to propanamide 
and ethanoic acid.
69. B. Nuclear charge increases faster 
than shielding
Across a period, the number of 
protons increases while electrons are 
added to the same principal energy 
level. Since shielding changes very 
little, the effective nuclear charge 
increases, pulling electrons closer to 
the nucleus.
70. D. N³⁻
Among these ions, N³⁻ has the largest 
ionic radius. Although all are 
isoelectronic (10 electrons), N³⁻ has 
the fewest protons, so its electrons 
are held least strongly, giving it the 
largest size.
71. C. Increased effective nuclear 
charge
Across a period, electrons are added 
to the same shell while nuclear 
charge increases. The stronger 
attraction between the nucleus and 
the outer electrons causes the atomic 
radius to decrease.
72. C. Group IA
Group IA (alkali metals) have one 
valence electron, which is easily 
removed because it is far from the 
nucleus and well shielded. 
Consequently, they possess the lowest 
first ionization energies.
73. B. The electron removed is a p￾electron
Beryllium has the stable 
configuration 2s², whereas boron is 
2s²2p¹. The 2p electron in boron is 
higher in energy and less tightly held 
than the 2s electron in beryllium, 
making it easier to remove.
74. B. Chlorine
Although fluorine is more 
electronegative, chlorine has the 
highest electron affinity because its 
larger atomic size reduces electron–
electron repulsion when an 
additional electron is added.
75. C. Aluminium
Aluminium is extracted by 
electrolysis of molten aluminium 
oxide dissolved in cryolite because it 
is too reactive to be reduced 
chemically by carbon or other 
reducing agents.
76. B. Propyne
In propyne (CH₃–C≡CH), the two carbon 
atoms involved in the triple bond are sp 
hybridized, while the methyl carbon is sp³ 
hybridized. The wording of the question is 
inaccurate because none of the options 
contain two sp² carbons and one sp 
carbon. Based on the available options, this 
appears to be the examiner’s intended 
answer, but the question is chemically 
flawed.
77. B. Lower melting point of Al₂O₃
Cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) lowers the melting 
point of aluminium oxide from about 
2050°C to approximately 950°C and 
also improves the electrical 
conductivity of the molten electrolyte.
78. C. Remove acidic impurities
Limestone (CaCO₃) decomposes to 
calcium oxide (CaO), which reacts 
with acidic silica impurities to form 
calcium silicate (CaSiO₃) slag that can 
be removed from the blast furnace.
79. C. CaSiO₃
Silica impurities react with calcium 
oxide to form calcium silicate 
(CaSiO₃), which floats on the molten 
iron as slag and is removed.
80. B. CO₂ + C → 2CO
Carbon monoxide, the main reducing 
agent in the blast furnace, is 
produced when carbon dioxide 
formed lower in the furnace reacts 
with hot coke:
CO₂ + C → 2CO.
81. C. Beryllium
Beryllium exhibits amphoteric 
behaviour, meaning its oxide (BeO) 
and hydroxide [Be(OH)₂] react with 
both acids and bases. This 
distinguishes it from the other 
alkaline earth metals, whose oxides 
and hydroxides are mainly basic.
82. B. Variable oxidation states
Transition metals commonly exhibit 
multiple oxidation states because both 
their ns and (n−1)d electrons can 
participate in bonding. This property 
contributes to their rich chemistry 
and formation of numerous compounds.
83. B. d–d transitions
The colours of transition metal 
complexes arise mainly from d–d 
electronic transitions. When visible 
light is absorbed, electrons are 
promoted between split d-orbitals, 
and the complementary colour is 
observed.
84. C. Zn²⁺
Zn²⁺ has a 3d¹⁰ electronic 
configuration with a completely filled 
d-subshell. Since no d–d transitions 
are possible, Zn²⁺ ions are colourless.
85. C. +3
In [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃, ammonia is a 
neutral ligand while the three 
chloride ions are outside the 
coordination sphere as Cl⁻. 
Therefore, cobalt must have an 
oxidation state of +3.
86. D. CO
Carbon monoxide (CO) is among the 
strongest field ligands in the 
spectrochemical series. It produces a 
very large crystal field splitting and 
commonly forms low-spin complexes.
87. C. Weak field ligands
Weak field ligands produce a small 
crystal field splitting energy (Δ₀), 
making it energetically favourable for 
electrons to occupy higher-energy 
orbitals rather than pair up. This 
results in high-spin complexes.
88. B. Number of unpaired electrons
The spin-only magnetic moment 
depends directly on the number of 
unpaired electrons present in the 
metal ion and is calculated using:
Mu = sqrt{n(n+2)}{ BM}
where n is the number of unpaired 
electrons.
89. C. Zn²⁺
Zn²⁺ has a completely filled d¹⁰ 
configuration, meaning all electrons 
are paired. Consequently, complexes 
containing Zn²⁺ are diamagnetic.
90. B. 4
The coordination number is the 
number of donor atoms directly 
bonded to the central metal ion. In 
[Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, four cyanide ligands 
coordinate to nickel, giving a 
coordination number of 4.
91. B. Tetracyanoplatinate(II)
Because the complex ion carries a 
negative charge, the metal name ends 
in –ate. Four cyanide ligands are 
named tetracyano-, and platinum is 
in the +2 oxidation state, giving 
tetracyanoplatinate(II).
92. C. 0
Carbon monoxide (CO) is a neutral 
ligand. Since the complex [Fe(CO)₅] 
is neutral overall, the oxidation state 
of iron must be 0.
93. C. Blue
A complex appears the 
complementary colour of the light it 
absorbs. Since orange light is 
absorbed, the observed colour is 
blue.
94. C. Unpaired electrons
Paramagnetism results from the 
presence of one or more unpaired 
electrons, which align with an 
external magnetic field and cause the 
substance to be attracted to the field.
95. D. Gold
Gold is one of the least reactive 
metals, occurring naturally in the 
elemental state because it resists 
oxidation and corrosion.
96. C. Sulphide ores
Froth flotation is specifically used to 
concentrate sulphide ores because 
sulphide particles are preferentially 
wetted by oil and carried to the 
surface by air bubbles.
97. B. Absence of air
Calcination involves heating an ore in 
the absence or limited supply of air, 
mainly to remove moisture and 
carbon dioxide from hydrated and 
carbonate ores.
98. B. Impure metal
During electrolytic refining, the 
impure metal serves as the anode, 
while a thin sheet of pure metal is the 
cathode. Pure metal is deposited on 
the cathode as electrolysis proceeds.
99. B. Similar atomic sizes
Transition metals readily form alloys 
because their atoms have similar 
atomic radii, allowing them to 
replace one another in metallic 
crystal lattices without significantly 
disturbing the structure.
100. C. Alkanone → oxime
In converting an alkanone to an 
oxime, the carbonyl carbon remains 
sp² hybridized. The reaction replaces 
the oxygen with a C=N–OH group 
without changing the hybridization of 
that carbon. The other 
transformations involve changes 
between sp³ and sp² hybridization.
