1. [TEST-01]
Answer: D
Answer text: Sound wave in air.

Explanation & Problem-Solving Strategy:

Core idea: A sound wave in air is a mechanical longitudinal wave. Air particles vibrate parallel to the direction in which the disturbance travels, producing compressions and rarefactions.

The other common examples do not fit this description: light is electromagnetic and transverse, while an ideal string wave is transverse. Sound in a solid can have both longitudinal and transverse components, but sound in air is longitudinal.

Similar questions: Classify a wave by asking whether the particles oscillate parallel or perpendicular to the propagation direction, and whether a material medium is required.

3. [TEST-03]
Answer: C
Answer text: When the string is perfectly flat.

Explanation & Problem-Solving Strategy:

Core idea: In a standing wave, particles at an extreme displacement momentarily have zero speed, so their energy is elastic potential energy. When the string is flat, displacement is zero and particle speed is greatest, so the energy is kinetic.

Thus the string is perfectly flat when the energy is entirely kinetic.

Similar questions: Locate the instant of maximum speed or maximum displacement before deciding whether the energy is kinetic or potential.
